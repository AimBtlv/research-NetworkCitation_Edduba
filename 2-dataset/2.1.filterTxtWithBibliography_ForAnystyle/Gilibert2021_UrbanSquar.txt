The Palace Squares
Ugarit’s western sector was dominated by the royal
palace compound. The architectural complex took on
its truly spectacular dimensions relatively late in the
city’s history, as a result of a mid-13th century redesign.
The new building compound came to cover over a
hectare and consisted, beyond the Palace itself, of sev-
eral annexes, including a small temple and a vast ban-
quet hall (the Bâtiment aux Piliers, or “Pillared Build-
ing”).80 The Royal Palace and its annexes were arranged
around a 500 m2 open-air space, conventionally called
the “Royal Plaza.”81 Despite the label, this space was
a ceremonial courtyard segregated from the rest of
the town and guarded by military checkpoints.82 Two
large public squares, which we may provisionally call
the “Square with the Basin” and the “Square with the
Vase,” were built outside the royal compound. These
squares were exclusively pedestrian spaces, lined by
