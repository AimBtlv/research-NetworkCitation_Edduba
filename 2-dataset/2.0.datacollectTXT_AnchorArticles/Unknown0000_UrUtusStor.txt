r-Utu hadn't seen the disaster coming. Now foreign
tribes were attacking the city. Panic flew through the
streets of Sippar. Everyone had to pack up and get out of
town. Even though he was a wealthy man and a priest, Ur-Utu
had no protection against the invaders. He must have alerted
his relatives while his servants wrapped the family's treasures
in rugs or cloth bags, ready to run. But before Ur-Utu aban-
doned his home, he rushed into his study where thousands
of written records were kept. How frantic he must have
been when he realized how little he could take with him!
Over the years, Ur-Utu had kept letters, contracts for
the sale of houses and fields, loan agreements, lists, prayers,
and even old school exercise tablets. He probably wanted to
take them all, but they were written on clay tablets, most of
which were about half an inch thick and about the size of a
grown man's hand—too heavy and bulky for him to carry
them all. Ur-Utu had to choose.
Because he could read, Ur-Utu was able to glance at
each document and quickly decide if it was important
enough to save. He tossed many on the floor and picked 49 People today keep the deed
